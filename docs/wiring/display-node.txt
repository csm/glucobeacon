Waveshare 7.5in e-Paper HAT (800x480 mono, UC8179, SPI3)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  CS       GPIO7   chip select
  SCK     GPIO38
  DIN      GPIO2   MOSI; panel is write-only
  DC       GPIO6   low = command
  RST      GPIO5   active low
  BUSY     GPIO4   LOW while refreshing
  No MISO: the panel never talks back.
  BUSY is LOW while refreshing - the opposite of most e-paper controllers.
  The controller wants 0 for black; the framebuffer uses 1. The driver inverts.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Buzzer, button LED, acknowledge button
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  BUZZ    GPIO15   MOSFET #1 SIG, active high
  LED     GPIO16   MOSFET #2 SIG, active high
  SW      GPIO47   to GND, active low, internal pull-up
  Drive the buzzer and LED LOW at boot, or the buzzer shrieks through resets.
  An SFM-27-W is an ACTIVE buzzer: on/off, no PWM needed.

  The button is on GPIO47, deliberately NOT GPIO0.
  GPIO0/3/45/46 are sampled at reset. A button held down through a reset on
  GPIO0 leaves the board in its serial bootloader: dark, silent, not alarming,
  with nothing to say what happened. The on-board PRG button is still on
  GPIO0 if a spare input helps during bring-up.

Changed from wiring diagram v1.0
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  * e-Paper SCK/MOSI/MISO were GPIO12/11/13 - those are the SX1262's RST,
    MISO and BUSY. The bus has moved to free pins.
  * e-Paper DC/CS were GPIO17/18 - those are the on-board OLED's I2C pins.
  * e-Paper MISO dropped entirely: the panel is write-only.
  * GPIO22/23 were listed as reserve. Neither exists on an ESP32-S3.

Changed in v1.2
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  * Acknowledge button moved GPIO0 -> GPIO47, and the e-Paper clock
    GPIO3 -> GPIO38. Both were strapping pins: sampled at reset, they change
    how the chip boots. GPIO0 is the dangerous one - a held button becomes a
    board that never starts. GPIO3 only selects the JTAG source, but a bus
    line has no pull at reset and there is no reason to leave a strap floating.
